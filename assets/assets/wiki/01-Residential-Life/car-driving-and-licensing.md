# Car, Driving and Licensing

Owning and driving a car in Japan is significantly different from most countries. Read this guide carefully before deciding to purchase a vehicle.

> **Note:** JICA-sponsored and JDS students are **not permitted to drive** in Japan per their sponsor's rules.

---

## Campus Parking Registration

All cars parked at IUJ (day-time or long-term) must be **registered with OSS**. All drivers must obtain an IUJ parking sticker. See [Car Registration](https://www2.iuj.ac.jp/oss/current-students/car-driving-and-licensing/registration/) for details.

---

## Valid Licenses for Driving in Japan

There are three recognized ways to drive legally in Japan:

### 1. Valid Japanese Driver's License
Obtained through the Japanese licensing process. See [Drivers License](https://www2.iuj.ac.jp/oss/current-students/car-driving-and-licensing/drivers-license/) for how to convert or obtain one.

### 2. International Driving Permit (IDP) — Geneva Convention Only
- Must carry BOTH the IDP and your passport at all times while driving
- Only IDPs issued under the **Geneva Convention** are valid in Japan
- IDPs from the Paris, Washington, or Vienna Conventions are **NOT recognized**
- Even if your country is a Geneva Convention signatory, you cannot drive with your home-country license alone — an IDP is required

### 3. Foreign License + Authorized Japanese Translation + Passport
This applies **only** to license holders from: Switzerland, Germany, France, Belgium, Monaco, and Taiwan.
- All three documents must be carried together at all times
- Translations must be issued by a consulate, JAF, or a government-designated entity (e.g., ZIPLUS Co., Ltd.)

---

## Validity Period

For options 2 and 3 above, the permitted driving period is **one year from your date of landing in Japan**, or until the permit/license expires — whichever comes first.

> Leaving and re-entering Japan within less than 3 months does **not** reset the one-year clock.

---

## Penalties for Unlicensed Driving

Driving without a valid license is a serious criminal offense under Japan's Road Traffic Act:

- Up to **3 years imprisonment** or a fine of up to **¥500,000** (or both)
- **25 demerit points** on the license point system — leads to immediate disqualification
- Insurance is **void** if driving unlicensed — you will be personally liable for all accident costs
- Can negatively affect **visa/residency status** and may result in deportation or visa denial

---

## Drunk Driving

Zero tolerance — IUJ students involved in drunk driving face scholarship cancellation and/or suspension/expulsion, in addition to Japan's strict criminal penalties. Passengers who allow a drunk person to drive are also held legally responsible.
