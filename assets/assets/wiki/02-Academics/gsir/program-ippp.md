# International Public Policy Program (IPPP)

## A One-Year Mid-Career Program for Diplomats and Government Officials

The International Public Policy Program (IPPP) is a **one-year mid-career program**, designed for **diplomats and government officials** of countries in Asia, the Pacific, and around the world. It is designed in collaboration with the **Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA)**.

Students choose a concentration area depending on their professional backgrounds and career goals:
- **International Affairs**
- **Public Policy**

The program encourages students to look back on and systematize their professional experiences, developing them into a body of knowledge with which they can further their professional capabilities. It also helps students develop the ability to think about and discuss various international public policy issues from long-term, broad, and diverse perspectives.

## Benefits

### Professional Skills
Acquire advanced professional skills to understand, analyze, and tackle complex international and public affairs.

### Career Development
The advanced knowledge and skills acquired will accelerate your career in your chosen profession.

### Leadership Enhancement
IUJ's multicultural learning environment will enhance participants' leadership skills.

## Message from the Program Director

**Hiroshi Kato, Professor and Program Director (Dean, GSIR)**

> "As we enter the third decade of the 21st century, we have witnessed ever-increasing globalized complexity and challenges in international and public affairs, world politics, and humanity as a whole that demand highly qualified, talented, and professional policy makers and practitioners. The International Public Policy Program is a mid-career professional education program designed to equip diplomats and government officials with skills and knowledge in the areas of international affairs and/or public policy and to enhance their leadership and performance in the most proficient manner. The IPPP is fully committed to producing scholarly diplomats and policy analysts."
