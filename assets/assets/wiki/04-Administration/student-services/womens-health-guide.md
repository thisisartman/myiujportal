# Women's Health Guide

## Menstrual Products

- **Pads** — widely available in all sizes at drugstores and convenience stores
- **Tampons** — less common; found at some convenience stores and drugstores
- International brands (OB, Tampax, Kotex): order via Amazon Japan
- **Disposal:** wrap in tissue or plastic bag; use sanitary bins in bathrooms or dorm trash bins

## Menstrual Pain Relief

Available over-the-counter (no prescription needed):
- **Eve (イブ)**
- **L-Pain 4 (エルペイン4)**

For stronger medication, visit a gynecologist.

## Gynecology Services

### Hitomi Ladies Clinic
- Location: near McDonald's in Muikamachi
- Female doctor who speaks English
- Accessible by city bus
- Website: https://hitomi-lc.com

### KIKAN Hospital
- English-speaking doctor available on **Fridays**
- First visit: **¥7,700** without a referral letter

### Pap Smears & Mammograms
- Available as part of the annual physical exam ("comprehensive health check") at **Yamato Hospital**
- Not covered by insurance unless medically indicated
- Done in private rooms with a curtain

> Note: **Moegi Clinic** and **Yamato Hospital** do not provide regular gynecology services.

## Birth Control

### Condoms
- Most common form of birth control in Japan
- Available at drugstores, convenience stores, the IUJ Shop
- Vending machines in SD2 and SD3 dorm restrooms
- Protects against pregnancy and STIs

### Emergency Contraception (Morning-After Pill)
- Available at **KIKAN Hospital**
- Must be taken **within 72 hours** of unprotected sex
- Cost: approximately **¥20,000**
- A follow-up visit is required after your next period (¥500–1,000)
- For emergencies only — not for regular use

### Other Options (The Pill & IUD)
- Birth control pills are typically prescribed for medical reasons only
- IUDs are becoming more available but remain limited
- Consult a doctor to discuss your options
