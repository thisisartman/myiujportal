# Hanko (Personal Seal)

A **hanko** (also called *inkan*) is a personal stamp used in Japan as a legal signature substitute. For major transactions in Japan — buying a car, signing a real estate contract, or transferring a phone contract — you may be required to use your **official registered seal** (*Jitsu-in*).

---

## When You Need a Hanko

- Purchasing a car
- Signing real estate contracts
- Transferring phone ownership to another person
- Any formal legal or administrative transaction requiring an official stamp

---

## Official Seal Registration (*Inkan Toroku*)

To use your seal officially, you must register it at the **City Office, Residential Affairs Division** (*Shimin-ka*).

- **Registration fee:** Free
- **Seal registration certificate** (*Inkan Toroku Shomeisho*): ¥300 per copy

### Seals That Cannot Be Registered

The following types of seals are ineligible:

1. A seal with a name that doesn't match your Residence Certificate
2. A seal made of rubber or other easily deformable material
3. A seal that is chipped or worn out
4. A seal without an outer border/line surrounding the name
5. A seal that cannot fit within a 20 mm × 20 mm square
6. A seal deemed generally inappropriate for registration

---

## Where to Get a Hanko

Online personal seal shops catering to foreign residents in Japan are available — search for ones that support non-Japanese names. OSS does not recommend specific vendors.

---

## If You Lose Your Seal or Registration Card

Report to the City Office (Residential Affairs Division / Shimin-ka) and submit an application to **invalidate your registered seal**. Do this promptly to prevent misuse.
