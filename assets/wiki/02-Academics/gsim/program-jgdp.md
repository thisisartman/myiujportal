# Japan-Global Development Program (JGDP)

**Duration:** 2 years | **Joint program:** GSIR and GSIM

## Overview

The JGDP provides students the opportunity to learn universal development and growth logic in politics, economy, and management, using Japan's experiences in economic development and corporate growth as case materials. It is not intended to train Japan specialists — rather, the logic and lessons from Japan's experience (successes and failures, 19th Century to present) help future leaders think strategically about their home countries' development. JGDP also enables Japanese students to explain their country's development logically when working abroad.

## Three Distinctive Features

### Non-Occidental Development Model
Japan's nation building from the 19th Century through continuous development in the 20th Century and sustainable growth in the 21st Century — with particular emphasis on approach, management style, and culture contrasted with European cases.

### Practical Knowledge
Skills and knowledge applicable to respective futures, based on Japan's past experience in policy making, economic development, and public and business management.

### Phases of Development
Japan's path from the Meiji Restoration (1868) to today covers almost every phase of development, which students can relate to their own home countries' stages.

## Program Director

**Gaku Funabashi**, Professor

*"Through JGDP, we hope that by linking Japan's experiences at different stages of development with knowledge in your own area of expertise, you will acquire the ability to think about strategies to take from situations you may encounter in the future."*

## JGDP Courses — GSIR

- Modern Japan in the World
- International Relations and Foreign Policy of Japan
- Japanese Development Cooperation: Implementation and Practice
- Postwar Japanese Politics
- Japanese Government and Politics
- Japan's Education System
- Japanese International Development Cooperation
- Politico-economic History of Japan's Modernization

## JGDP Courses — GSIM

- Agriculture Business and Policy in Japan
- Energy Policy and Business Leadership
- Japanese Style Management and Corporate Governance
- Japan's Major Industries and Human Resource Practices
- Monozukuri (Manufacturing) Management in Japan
- Small to Medium-sized Firms in Japan

In addition to JGDP courses, students must take courses required by their respective concentration.
